A statutory register you can stand over

Append-only records. Check-ins and check-outs can never be edited or deleted — not even by an administrator. Every entry carries who recorded it and when, and nobody can act as somebody else.

The day closes itself. At local midnight — the site's own timezone, computed on the server — anyone required but not seen becomes a recorded miss. A tablet with a mis-set clock can't shift what "today" means.

GDPR is handled properly. One click produces a resident's complete file for a subject access request. Departed residents can be erased on schedule, with the erasure itself logged. Data lives in Frankfurt, encrypted in transit and at rest.

Roles that match the job. Staff record. Supervisors also manage the resident register. Admins also manage accounts and run GDPR export and erasure — and every rule is enforced in the database, not just the screen.

Common questions

What does CheckSteady do?

It replaces the paper register in residential sites. Staff record one daily welfare check-in per resident and every door movement; the day closes itself at local midnight and missed check-ins are recorded automatically. It also runs fire roll calls across every warden's phone at once.

Can records be edited afterwards?

No. Check-ins, movements and drills are append-only and cannot be edited or deleted by anyone, including an administrator. Every entry carries who recorded it and when.

Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes. Check-ins and movements queue on the device and send themselves when the connection returns. The roll call marks people safe offline and merges the marks across phones afterwards.

Is there a free trial?

Yes — a free trial of a single site with no card, which you can start with sample residents to explore or empty and ready for your own list. When a trial ends the site becomes read-only rather than being deleted.

How much does CheckSteady cost?

It is priced per site, per month, with every feature included and no charge per resident or per staff account.
