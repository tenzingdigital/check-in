You are the controller. We are the processor. You decide the purposes and means

## 2. Our instructions

If we believe an instruction infringes the GDPR, we will tell you promptly and
may pause that processing until it is resolved.

We may process data without your specific instruction where EU or Irish law
requires it. Where we may lawfully say so, we will tell you before we do.

Everyone we authorise to process your data is bound by a duty of confidentiality
that survives the end of their engagement, and is given access only to what
their work requires.

Access by us is limited to what support and maintenance genuinely need. We do
not browse customer registers.

We implement the technical and organisational measures set out in **Annex II**,
which is a description of what the system actually does rather than a statement
of intent. We keep those measures under review and will not reduce them during
the term.

## 5. Sub-processors

You give us **general authorisation** to engage the sub-processors listed in

Before adding or replacing a sub-processor we will give you **thirty days'**
notice by email. If you reasonably object on data protection grounds within that
period, we will work with you to find a solution; if we cannot, you may end the
agreement without penalty and receive a pro-rata refund of fees paid in advance.

We impose data protection obligations on each sub-processor that are no less

## 6. International transfers

Personal data is stored and processed in the **European Union** (Frankfurt,
Germany).

Our hosting sub-processor is a company incorporated outside the EEA. Where any

European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, together with any

## 7. Helping you meet your obligations

**Data subject rights (Articles 12–23).** Taking into account the nature of the
processing, we will help you respond to requests from data subjects. The service
provides one-click export of everything held about an individual resident, and a
one-click erasure that removes their record and history while logging that the
erasure took place. If a data subject contacts us directly, we will not respond
substantively — we will pass it to you promptly.

**Breach notification (Articles 33–34).** We will notify you of a personal data
breach affecting your data **without undue delay and in any event within 48
hours** of becoming aware of it. The notification will describe what happened,
the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records affected, the
likely consequences, and the measures taken. We will help you meet your own
notification duties to the Data Protection Commission and to data subjects. It
is for you, as controller, to make those notifications.

**Impact assessments (Articles 35–36).** We will give you the information you
reasonably need to carry out a Data Protection Impact Assessment and, if needed,
to consult the Data Protection Commission.

**A note we would rather state than leave you to discover.** Recording the daily
presence of residents in accommodation is systematic monitoring of individuals
who are frequently vulnerable, and the identity numbers involved can reveal a
person's immigration status. A DPIA is very likely to be required before you
begin. Annex I and Annex II are written to be usable as evidence within one.

## 8. Audit

We will make available the information reasonably needed to demonstrate our
compliance with this agreement, and allow and contribute to audits by you or an
auditor you appoint.

Audits are on **thirty days'** written notice, during business hours, no more

other customers. You bear your own audit costs.

## 9. Deletion and return

On the end of the agreement, or earlier on your written request, we will
**delete or return** your personal data at your choice, and delete existing
copies, unless EU or Irish law requires us to keep them.

Unless you tell us otherwise: a lapsed trial is retained read-only for thirty
days and then deleted; a cancelled subscription is retained for thirty days and
then deleted. Backups are overwritten on their own cycle and are deleted no
later than **thirty-five days** after the data is deleted from the live service.

Separately, the service enforces the retention periods you configure. The
default register retention is **180 days**, matching the six-month limit in the
IPAS verification policy of March 2026; granular event logs default to 90 days.
These run automatically.

## 10. Liability

# Annex I — What is processed

**Subject matter.** Provision of the CheckSteady daily welfare register and door
log.

**Duration.** For the term of the agreement, plus the deletion periods in
section 9.

**Nature and purpose.** Recording that a resident presented at a site on a given
day; recording entry and exit; producing lists of who has not been seen;
reporting counts against thresholds the controller configures; producing
registers and exports for the controller.

**Categories of data subject**

- Residents of the controller's accommodation, who may include people in the
  international protection process and other people in vulnerable circumstances.
- The controller's staff who use the service.

**Types of personal data**

| Data subject | Data |
|---|---|
| Residents | Name; date of birth; identity document type and number (TRC or IRP); registration and departure dates; a record of each day they presented, or did not, with time and recording staff member; entry and exit events |
| Staff | Name; email address; role; account status; login and session records; IP address at login |

**Special category data.** The service has no field for Article 9 data, and the
controller is instructed not to enter any. The controller should note that an
identity document number issued only to international protection applicants may
indirectly reveal immigration status, and should treat the register accordingly.

**Children.** The service excludes residents under 18 from the verification
process automatically. Records of minors may exist where the controller has
registered them.

# Annex II — Technical and organisational measures

These describe the system as built.

**Access control**
- Every user has an individual account with a role of guard, supervisor or
  administrator. There is no shared or anonymous access, and no public sign-up
  to an existing customer's data.
- Authorisation is enforced inside the database by row-level security policies
  and by permission checks inside privileged functions, not by the user
  interface. Hiding a control in the browser is never the only barrier.
- Functions take the acting user's identity from the authenticated session, not
  from a parameter, so no request can be crafted to act as another user.
- Staff hold no direct read access to the residents table, which holds dates of
  birth; they read a restricted view that never exposes one.
- Sessions expire after 12 hours by default. Deactivating an account or changing
  a password ends every existing session immediately.

**Cryptography**
- All traffic is over TLS. Data is encrypted at rest by the hosting provider.
- Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes with a work factor of 12.
- Session tokens and password-reset tokens are stored only as SHA-256 digests, so
  a database backup contains no credential that can be replayed.

**Integrity of the record**
- Check-in and movement records are append-only. No role, including
  administrator, can edit or delete them. Removal occurs only through the logged
  erasure process or the scheduled retention purge.
- Every record carries the staff member who created it, and the database refuses
  to delete a staff identity that historical records depend on.

**Data minimisation**
- The service holds only the fields listed in Annex I. Free-text fields and room
  identifiers were deliberately removed.
- Identity numbers are displayed only on an individual's detail view, never in
  lists.

**Resilience and separation**
- Hosted in the European Union (Frankfurt). Managed daily backups with
  point-in-time recovery.
- The database rejects connections from the public internet.
- The application loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers; a
  content-security policy permits only its own code, admitted by hash.

**Deletion**
- Retention periods are enforced by a scheduled job, not by manual effort.
- Erasure of an individual removes their record and history and writes a
  non-identifying log entry proving the erasure occurred.

**Organisational**
- Personnel are bound by confidentiality obligations.
- Changes are version-controlled and pass an automated test suite covering the
  authorisation model before deployment.
