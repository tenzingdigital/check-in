## What changed in this version

Version 2026-09-03 described the service as it stood before several features
were added. The corrections below are factual, and each was checked against the
running code rather than against our own notes.

1. **Annex I now states that the service holds special category data.** The
   previous version said there was no field for Article 9 data. That was wrong.
   Where the controller turns the evacuation feature on, an evacuation
   assistance need is recorded against a resident, and that is data concerning
   health. The reason for an authorised absence, which may be *medical*, is
   assessed in the same section.
2. **Annex I's list of resident data is rewritten.** Rooms and room history,
   household links, authorised absences, breach reports issued to IPAS,
   roll-call marks, the nightly overnight-absence record and door-recorded
   check-ins were all missing from it.
3. **Annex I adds two categories of data subject** the previous version did not
   mention: visitors, contractors and suppliers signed in at the door, and the
   controller's own site staff who appear on the staff list without holding an
   account.
4. **Annex II's minimisation paragraph is rewritten.** Its two claims — that
   free-text fields and room identifiers had been removed — are both now false.
   Room identifiers returned, and the service has seven capped free-text
   fields, each of which is now named.
5. **Annex III corrects what leaves by email.** The previous version said
   resident data is never sent by email. That is still true of the Sunday
   Weekly register update, which states counts and a link only, to the
   centre's own staff ticked to receive it. It is not true of the nightly
   House Rules reminder, which carries resident names and room labels to
   the centre's own supervisors and administrators. The sub-processor's
   stated purpose is widened to match.
6. **Smaller corrections.** The under-18 exemption follows a configurable adult
   age, 18 by default, not a fixed 18. Annex II now records the second
   authentication step and the separation of customers into their own database
   schemas, both of which were in place but undescribed. Section 9 names the
   retention that applies to the records added since the last version.

## 1. Roles

You are the controller. We are the processor. You decide the purposes and means
of processing residents' and staff personal data in CheckSteady; we process it
only to provide the service to you.

You confirm that you have a lawful basis for the processing, that you have given
data subjects the information Articles 13 and 14 require, and that your
instructions to us do not require us to breach data protection law.

## 2. Our instructions

We process personal data **only on your documented instructions**, including on
international transfers. Your instructions are: these terms, the Data Processing
Agreement, your configuration of the service, and anything you ask us to do in
writing.

Your configuration is a real instruction and not a formality. Several of the
categories in Annex I exist only where you switch a feature on, and which of
your own supervisors and administrators receive the Weekly register update
is a tick you make on their staff record.

If we believe an instruction infringes the GDPR, we will tell you promptly and
may pause that processing until it is resolved.

We may process data without your specific instruction where EU or Irish law
requires it. Where we may lawfully say so, we will tell you before we do.

## 3. Confidentiality

Everyone we authorise to process your data is bound by a duty of confidentiality
that survives the end of their engagement, and is given access only to what
their work requires.

Access by us is limited to what support and maintenance genuinely need. We do
not browse customer registers.

## 4. Security (Article 32)

We implement the technical and organisational measures set out in **Annex II**,
which is a description of what the system actually does rather than a statement
of intent. Where a measure depends on your staff rather than on the software,
Annex II says so instead of claiming the software prevents it. We keep those
measures under review and will not reduce them during the term.

## 5. Sub-processors

You give us **general authorisation** to engage the sub-processors listed in
**Annex III**.

Before adding or replacing a sub-processor we will give you **thirty days'**
notice by email. If you reasonably object on data protection grounds within that
period, we will work with you to find a solution; if we cannot, you may end the
agreement without penalty and receive a pro-rata refund of fees paid in advance.

We impose data protection obligations on each sub-processor that are no less
protective than those in this agreement, and we remain fully liable to you for
their performance.

## 6. International transfers

Personal data is stored and processed in the **European Union** (Frankfurt,
Germany).

Our hosting sub-processor is a company incorporated outside the EEA. Where any
transfer of personal data outside the EEA occurs, or where personnel outside the
EEA could access it, it is carried out under an adequacy decision or under the
European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, together with any
supplementary measures required following the transfer impact assessment we
maintain. We will provide the current transfer documentation on request.

## 7. Helping you meet your obligations

**Data subject rights (Articles 12–23).** Taking into account the nature of the
processing, we will help you respond to requests from data subjects. The service
provides one-click export of everything held about an individual resident, and a
one-click erasure that removes their record and history while logging that the
erasure took place. If a data subject contacts us directly, we will not respond
substantively — we will pass it to you promptly.

**Breach notification (Articles 33–34).** We will notify you of a personal data
breach affecting your data **without undue delay and in any event within 48
hours** of becoming aware of it. The notification will describe what happened,
the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records affected, the
likely consequences, and the measures taken. We will help you meet your own
notification duties to the Data Protection Commission and to data subjects. It
is for you, as controller, to make those notifications.

**Impact assessments (Articles 35–36).** We will give you the information you
reasonably need to carry out a Data Protection Impact Assessment and, if needed,
to consult the Data Protection Commission.

**A note we would rather state than leave you to discover.** Recording the daily
presence of residents in accommodation is systematic monitoring of individuals
who are frequently vulnerable. Three things in the record carry more meaning
than they appear to: the identity numbers involved can reveal a person's
immigration status; an authorised absence recorded with the reason *interview*
records that a resident attended an international protection interview; and,
where you turn the evacuation feature on, the evacuation assistance need is
Article 9 data. A DPIA is very likely to be required before you begin, and
should address all three. Annex I and Annex II are written to be usable as
evidence within one.

## 8. Audit

We will make available the information reasonably needed to demonstrate our
compliance with this agreement, and allow and contribute to audits by you or an
auditor you appoint.

Audits are on **thirty days'** written notice, during business hours, no more
than once a year unless a breach or a regulator requires otherwise, subject to
confidentiality, and conducted so as not to disrupt the service or the data of
other customers. You bear your own audit costs.

## 9. Deletion and return

On the end of the agreement, or earlier on your written request, we will
**delete or return** your personal data at your choice, and delete existing
copies, unless EU or Irish law requires us to keep them.

Unless you tell us otherwise: a lapsed trial is retained read-only for thirty
days and then deleted; a cancelled subscription is retained for thirty days and
then deleted. Backups are overwritten on their own cycle and are deleted no
later than **thirty-five days** after the data is deleted from the live service.

Separately, the service enforces the retention periods you configure. The
default register retention is **180 days**, matching the six-month limit in the
IPAS verification policy of March 2026; granular event logs default to 90 days.
These run automatically as part of a nightly job, and each run is recorded, so a
purge that stops running is visible rather than silent.

The records added since the previous version follow those same two periods:
authorised absences, breach reports issued, roll calls and their marks, the
nightly overnight-absence record and the administrators' audit trail are kept
for the register period; visitor, contractor and supplier sign-ins are kept for
the event period. Two records deliberately do not expire on a clock:

- **Room history.** A resident's record of which room they occupied, and when,
  is kept for as long as their record exists and is removed with it, on erasure
  or on deletion of the account. It is not purged on a timer.
- **The staff list.** The controller's own site staff are archived rather than
  deleted, so that a past roll call or door sign-in that names a person keeps
  its meaning.

## 10. Liability

Liability under this agreement is subject to the limits in section 9 of the
Terms of Service, except where those limits are not permitted by law. Nothing
here limits a data subject's rights or a supervisory authority's powers.

# Annex I — What is processed

**Subject matter.** Provision of the CheckSteady daily welfare register and door
log.

**Duration.** For the term of the agreement, plus the deletion periods in
section 9.

**Nature and purpose.** Recording that a resident presented at a site on a given
day; recording entry and exit at the door; producing lists of who has not been
seen; recording who was off site overnight; recording absences the controller
has authorised, and reports the controller has issued to IPAS; accounting for
everyone on site during a fire drill or an incident; recording where residents
sleep and where they have slept; reporting counts against thresholds the
controller configures; producing registers, reports and exports for the
controller, including two recurring emails described in Annex III.

**Categories of data subject**

- Residents of the controller's accommodation, who may include people in the
  international protection process and other people in vulnerable circumstances.
- The controller's staff who use the service and hold an account.
- The controller's site staff who do not hold an account, and who appear on the
  site staff list so that they can be signed in at the door in one action and
  named at a roll call.
- Visitors, contractors and suppliers signed in and out at the door, where the
  controller turns the visitors feature on.

Recipients of the Weekly register update are not a separate category: the
service offers the tick that adds someone only to a supervisor or an
administrator who already has an account, so they are already covered
above.

**Types of personal data**

| Data subject | Data |
|---|---|
| Residents | Name; date of birth; identity document type and number (TRC or IRP); registration and departure dates and status. Where the controller uses rooms: the room the resident occupies, and the history of every room they have occupied with the dates and the staff member who moved them. Where the controller uses households: a reference linking family members, and nothing else — no family name and no statement of relationship. Where the controller uses the evacuation feature: an evacuation assistance need, as one code from a fixed list (see *Special category data* below). A record of each day the resident presented, or did not, with the time and the recording staff member; entry and exit events at the door, including those recorded on a terminal during a connection outage and sent later, which are marked as such; a nightly record of whether the resident was off site at the end of the site day and when they were last seen leaving; a mark recording that the resident was accounted for at a drill or an incident, by whom and when; authorised absences, being the first and last day away, a reason from a fixed list, whether a parent or guardian agreed where the resident is a child, who approved it and the day it was cut short if it was; and the fact that a breach report was issued to IPAS, being its kind (house rules, or misuse of the verification system), the day, the staff member who recorded it and the report's reference. No account of what happened is held with a breach report |
| Staff who use the service | Name; email address; role; account status; login and session records, including the IP address at login, the country that address resolved to, the browser reported, and why a login was treated as unusual. Where the controller requires a code at login: the emailed code, held only as a digest, and a marker that a browser has been trusted |

**Special category data.** The previous version of this agreement said the
service had no field for Article 9 data. That was wrong, and this version
corrects it.

- **Evacuation assistance need.** Where you turn the evacuation feature on, each
  resident carries one code from a fixed list: none, needs help to move, needs
  help to hear the alarm, needs help to find the way, has an infant or is a
  carer, or other with no detail held. It exists because a centre has a
  fire-safety duty to know who needs help to get out, and because a personal
  emergency evacuation plan is what a fire officer expects. **This is Article 9
  data.** A centre that turns the feature on is processing data concerning
  health and must record that in its DPIA. Article 9(2)(c), protecting the vital
  interests of the data subject, together with your statutory fire-safety duty,
  are the conditions we expect you to record. The mitigations built around it
  are that it is a code and not free text, so nothing beyond the code can
  arrive; that it appears only on the roll call, the evacuation list and room
  occupancy; that it is not searchable and does not appear on the gate or
  register cards; and that it is absent entirely where the feature is off.
- **The reason for an authorised absence.** The reason is one of holiday,
  family, medical, interview, education, work or other. Our assessment is that
  *medical* should be treated as data concerning health, and therefore as
  Article 9 data: it reveals that the resident sought or received healthcare on
  identified days, even though it says nothing about any condition. We would
  rather tell you that and be over-cautious than have you find it in a DPIA
  review. It is a single code, held only where a supervisor records the absence.
  If your own assessment reaches a different conclusion, record the reasoning.
- **Data that is sensitive by inference rather than by Article 9.** A TRC or IRP
  number is not itself Article 9 data, but a number issued only to international
  protection applicants can reveal immigration status. An absence recorded with
  the reason *interview* records attendance at an international protection
  interview. Neither is a special category under Article 9, and both should be
  treated with the same care as if they were.
- **Records that are adverse but not Article 10 data.** A breach report records
  only that a report was issued to IPAS, of which of two kinds, on what day, by
  whom, and its reference. It is not a record of a criminal conviction or
  offence within Article 10. It is nonetheless adverse information about an
  identified person, and your DPIA should say how it is used and who may see it.

There is no photograph, no biometric, and no field for a narrative about a
resident. See Annex II for where free text does exist in the service and what it
is for.

**Children.** The service excludes residents below the site's configured adult
age from the daily verification requirement automatically. That age is 18 by
default and is a setting you control. The age is computed as of the day being
evaluated, so a person is never treated as an adult for a day on which they were
a child. Records of children may exist where you have registered them, and where
you record an authorised absence for a child the service records whether a
parent or guardian agreed. The service marks a resident below the adult age as
a child on screen and in the Weekly register update viewed or downloaded under
Admin → Reports; the Sunday email carries no resident data at all, so that
marker never leaves by email.

# Annex II — Technical and organisational measures

These describe the system as built. Where something is instructed rather than
enforced, it is described as instructed.

**Access control**
- Every user has an individual account with a role of guard, supervisor or
  administrator. There is no shared or anonymous access, and no public sign-up
  to an existing customer's data. Self-serve sign-up creates a new, empty
  customer and nothing else.
- Authorisation is enforced inside the database by row-level security policies
  and by permission checks inside privileged functions, not by the user
  interface. Hiding a control in the browser is never the only barrier.
- Functions take the acting user's identity from the authenticated session, not
  from a parameter, so no request can be crafted to act as another user.
- Staff hold no direct read access to the residents table, which holds dates of
  birth; they read a restricted view that never exposes one.
- Sessions expire after 12 hours by default. Deactivating an account or changing
  a password ends every existing session immediately.
- Where you require it, a supervisor's or administrator's login takes a second
  step: a code sent by email. Sign-ins are recorded with the country the address
  resolved to, and a senior login from outside the countries you nominate can be
  refused. Where a user chooses to trust a browser, that browser may skip the
  code for thirty days; the marker is stored only as a digest, it is not a
  session and it does not extend one.

**Separation of customers**
- Each customer's data lives in its own PostgreSQL schema rather than behind a
  customer identifier on shared tables, so isolation is structural. A query that
  fails to name the right schema fails outright rather than quietly returning
  another customer's residents.

**Cryptography**
- All traffic is over TLS. Data is encrypted at rest by the hosting provider.
- Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes with a work factor of 12.
- Session tokens, password-reset tokens, login codes and trusted-browser markers
  are stored only as SHA-256 digests, so a database backup contains no
  credential that can be replayed.
- Where a terminal is used offline, the copy of the register it holds and the
  queue of events it has not yet sent are encrypted in the browser's storage.
  The key exists only in the open tab, the register copy is cleared at logout,
  and the queue is cleared once sent.

**Integrity of the record**
- Check-in and movement records are append-only. No role, including
  administrator, can edit or delete them. Removal occurs only through the logged
  erasure process or the scheduled retention purge.
- Every record carries the staff member who created it, and the database refuses
  to delete a staff identity that historical records depend on.
- An event recorded during an outage and sent later carries both the time it
  happened and the time the server received it, and is marked as a late entry,
  so it is never presented as if it had been recorded live.
- Changes made by administrators and supervisors to residents, staff and
  settings are written to an audit trail with the row before and after. Nobody
  can alter that trail.
- Opening a resident's record is itself recorded: who opened it, from which
  screen, and when.

**Data minimisation**
- The service holds only the fields listed in Annex I. There is no photograph,
  no biometric, no phone number and no vehicle registration.
- **A resident's own record contains no free-text field.** Every value in it is
  a name, a date, an identity document number, a reference to a room or a
  household, or a single code from a fixed list. The fixed lists are enforced by
  database constraints, so a value outside a list cannot be stored at all, by
  any route.
- **Identity numbers are displayed only on an individual's detail view, never in
  a list, and never in an email.** This is enforced by the views staff read and
  asserted by the automated test suite.
- **Room identifiers are in use again.** The previous version of this agreement
  said they had been removed. What was removed, in an earlier change, was a
  free-text room reference typed into the resident's record. A room is now a
  reference to a room a supervisor has created, with a building, a floor and a
  number.
- **Free text exists in seven places, and we would rather list them than claim
  it does not.** A field that accepts free text is a field into which sensitive
  detail can be typed by a member of your staff. None of these is about a
  resident's circumstances, and each is capped in the database except where
  noted:

  | Where | Cap | What it is for |
  |---|---|---|
  | A note on a room | 120 characters | Why a room is out of use, or what a vacancy is. Viewed and downloaded under Admin → Reports; it does not leave the service by email (Annex III) |
  | A room's bed configuration | 80 characters | How a room is laid out. Also viewed and downloaded under Admin → Reports; it does not leave the service by email |
  | The reference a breach report carries | 60 characters | The IPAS or file reference of a report that was issued, not a description of what happened |
  | A line written when a drill or evacuation ends | 200 characters | The fire-safety record: which alarm, which assembly point, what went wrong |
  | A visitor's company, or the reason for the call | 80 characters | Identifying a caller at the door |
  | A site staff member's job title | 80 characters | Naming the role on the staff list |
  | The reason an administrator types when exporting or erasing a resident's record | No cap | Accountability for the export or the erasure. This one is retained: the export reason on the audit trail, the erasure reason in the erasure log, which is not purged |

  The database constrains the length of these fields and what they are attached
  to. It cannot constrain what a person types into them. **You should instruct
  your staff that no health, immigration, religious or other sensitive detail
  about a person is to be typed into any of them**, and that the erasure reason
  in particular survives the erasure it describes.

**Resilience and separation**
- Hosted in the European Union (Frankfurt). Managed daily backups with
  point-in-time recovery, and an encrypted off-provider copy kept for
  thirty-five days.
- The database rejects connections from the public internet.
- The application loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers; a
  content-security policy permits only its own code, admitted by hash.
- The country a login came from is resolved from a table bundled with the
  application, not by asking a third-party service, so a sign-in does not
  disclose an IP address to anyone outside the sub-processors in Annex III.

**Deletion**
- Retention periods are enforced by a scheduled job, not by manual effort. Each
  run is recorded and a job that stops running is visible in the service.
- Erasure of an individual removes their record, their events, their register
  rows, their room history, their absences, their breach reports, their
  overnight-absence rows, their roll-call marks, the record of who viewed their
  file and their audit rows, and writes a non-identifying log
  entry proving the erasure occurred: a digest of the internal identifier, the
  number of rows removed, the reason given, the administrator and the time.

**Organisational**
- Personnel are bound by confidentiality obligations.
- Changes are version-controlled and pass an automated test suite covering the
  authorisation model before deployment.
- The list of people who hold direct access to the database outside the
  application is written down and reviewed quarterly.

# Annex III — Sub-processors

| Sub-processor | Purpose | Location of data |
|---|---|---|
| Render Services, Inc. | Application hosting, managed PostgreSQL database, backups, the nightly scheduled job | Frankfurt, Germany (EU) |
| Resend, Inc. | Transactional email: staff invitations, password resets and login codes; the nightly House Rules reminder, which carries resident data to the controller's own supervisors and administrators; the Sunday Weekly register update, which carries no resident data, to the controller's own staff ticked to receive it | EU/US — see section 6 |

**What leaves the service by email.** The previous version of this agreement
said that resident data is never sent by email. That is still true of the
Sunday Weekly register update below; it is not true of the House Rules
reminder, and this version corrects it.

- **Invitations, password resets and login codes** carry no resident data: the
  staff member's own address, their name, and a single-use link or code.
- **The nightly House Rules reminder**, where you turn it on, goes to your own
  active supervisors and administrators. It lists each resident at or over a
  figure you set, by name and room label, with two counts against your House
  Rules settings — consecutive nights missed, and nights missed within the
  configured window. It is sent only on nights there is somebody to list.
- **The Sunday Weekly register update**, where you turn it on — and at any time
  an administrator chooses to send it by hand from Settings, whether the weekly
  schedule is on or off — goes to your own supervisors and administrators
  ticked "Gets the Sunday report" on their staff record. The tick is offered
  to nobody else, since only a supervisor or an administrator may run the
  report it summarises; demoting someone off those roles clears it
  automatically. **It carries no resident data.** It states a count for
  resident absences, weekend updates, removals and room updates, and a link
  into the app; the names, rooms and dates behind those counts stay in the
  app, under Admin → Reports, where they can also be downloaded as a
  spreadsheet.

Only the House Rules reminder carries resident data, and it carries no date
of birth, identity document number, evacuation need, absence reason or free
text about a person — a name, a room label and the two night counts above,
nothing more. The Sunday email carries none of that either: no resident
data leaves the service by it at all.

Choosing which of your own staff receive the Weekly register update is your
instruction under section 2. It is a role decision, not a disclosure
decision: every recipient already holds a supervisor or administrator
account, and the message itself carries no resident data.
